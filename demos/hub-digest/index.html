What an email has to pass

Each rule is run on the finished email, not on the data it was made from.

  • ✓Small enough not to be cut off (passes)17.4 KB. Gmail cuts an email off above 102.0 KB.
  • ✓A subject that fits an inbox (passes)57 characters, and 60 is about what a phone shows.
  • ✓A line to follow the subject (passes)108 characters, hidden at the top of the email for the inbox to show.
  • ✓No pictures to be blocked (passes)Many inboxes hide pictures until asked. There are none.
  • ✓No script, form or frame (passes)Email programs strip them, and some treat them as a threat.
  • ✓Nothing fetched from elsewhere (passes)No web fonts, no style sheets, no tracking pixel.
  • ✓Every link is secure or filled in by the sender (passes)10 links.
  • ✓A way to stop the emails, and who sent them (passes)UK rules ask for both in any marketing email.
  • ✓Layout by tables that screen readers skip (passes)12 tables, each marked as layout only.
  • ✓Only styling that Outlook and Gmail keep (passes)No flex, grid, positioning or variables: tables, padding and plain colours.
  • ✓Text large enough to read on a phone (passes)Reading text is 14 px or more. Labels in capitals are 12 px.
  • ✓Every colour pair is easy to read (passes)The weakest pair is 7.9 to 1, and 4.5 is the mark. 8 colours are used.
  • ✓Said in the right language, with a title (passes)A screen reader is told the language, and the title is the subject.
  • ✓A plain-text version that says the same (passes)89 lines, none over 78 characters but for links, and every link in the email is in it.

Behind the reconstruction

The documented event

An original interpretation of Super Heavy’s return and first tower catch during SpaceX Starship Flight 5, 13 October 2024. Mission operator and reference-footage owner: SpaceX. This is an independent study, not an official simulator or SpaceX product.

SpaceX — Starship’s fifth flight test ↗ Official mission page / webcast reference. Opens externally; no reference media is loaded here.
Timing honesty: the initial anchors are approximate mission-elapsed visual targets, nominally ±2 seconds, and have not been frame-verified in this file. They are not video-player timestamps. The scene hits these targets exactly; this does not establish frame-accurate agreement with the webcast. Verify or calibrate them before using this as an audited reconstruction.

Three-event timing sheet

Enter mission-elapsed times as MM:SS.s. All three views, the vehicle, arms, plume and event markers retime together. The timeline always ends at capture and spans the preceding 40 seconds. “Tower-crown crossing” means the model booster’s upper rim reaches the tower’s height; it is an editorial cue, not an official telemetry event.

One scene, not three separate animations

A single deterministic clock drives the whole reconstruction. Scrubbing evaluates the scene directly rather than advancing a physics simulation. Wide, booster-mounted and catch-detail cameras therefore show the same vehicle and mechanism state. The onboard view is attached to the vehicle’s model transform.

The roughly 71 m × 9 m booster and 146 m tower establish scale. The descent curve, capture elevation, lug geometry, arm movement, engine transitions, landscaping, atmosphere and camera positions are illustrative rather than engineering measurements. No telemetry or official CAD is used. The initial paused view is a six-seconds-to-capture preview; Replay starts the complete approach.

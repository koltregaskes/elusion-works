Elusion Works/Demos/The Catch Is You

An essay as a film of a minute and a half

The catch is you.

Kol’s Korner opinion essay “AI’s Efficiency Promise Has a Catch - You”, turned into a short explainer. Every line on screen is taken word for word from the essay, though many are cut out of longer sentences. The film was written as code and rendered with HyperFrames.

  • 1 minute 34 seconds
  • Music, no speech
  • Every word is on screen and in the transcript

On a phone held upright, the film’s smaller lines are too small to read comfortably. Turn the phone sideways and use full screen, or read the transcript below, which has every line.

Transcript

Press a time to play the film from that line.

  1. AI’s efficiency promise has a catch.
  2. You.
  3. Every new AI tool promises the same thing. Work smarter. Save time. Focus on what matters.
  4. Here’s what actually happens.
  5. You do complete tasks faster.
  6. Then your manager notices.
  7. The efficiency gains don’t free up your time. They raise the baseline.
  8. This isn’t new. The Jevons paradox.
  9. Coal-fired engines didn’t reduce coal consumption. Consumption exploded.
  10. AI does this to your capacity.
  11. You’re the coal.
  12. 2x faster at writing emails?
  13. You don’t write half as many. You write twice as many.
  14. Your output doubled. Your capacity didn’t.
  15. What actually helps. You need boundaries. 1. Define “done” before you start. Set the scope up front. Stop when you hit it.
  16. 2. Track what you eliminate, not what you produce. Measure time saved, not tasks completed.
  17. 3. Push back on scope creep. When someone says “but with AI you can just...”, the answer is “yes, and I’ve already allocated that capacity elsewhere.”
  18. 4. Use efficiency gains for recovery, not more work. If AI saves you two hours, spend one of them not working.
  19. Decouple your value from your velocity.
  20. If your role is purely about speed and volume, AI will eventually do it better than you.
  21. The people who thrive aren’t the ones doing more.
  22. They’re the ones deciding what’s worth doing at all.
  23. That’s the skill to invest in.

How it was made

  • Words. Cut down from Kol Tregaskes’s opinion essay, published on Kol’s Korner in February 2026. Every line is the essay’s own wording; nothing was added. It is one person’s argument, not a study.
  • Pictures. Seven scenes, each one a small web page: type, a few drawn shapes and timed moves. No stock footage, no generated images. The bars and meters marked 1× and 2× illustrate the essay’s own example; they are not measured data.
  • Timing. One hundred beats a minute. Every scene starts on a bar, and the hard lines land on the beat.
  • Music. A quiet backing track made by a short script, so nothing is borrowed and nothing needs a licence.
  • Checks. HyperFrames’ own checker passes with no errors and no warnings: structure, layout, contrast, reading time and sound.

The film’s source is in this demo’s tools/film folder, which is not served with the page: see it in the site’s repository. Change a line of text there and render it again.
